Acknowledgements

Jolito is built with genuine gratitude on the shoulders of generous creators, open communities, and open-source software.

🌮 The Living Spanish Language

  • Wiktionary & Kaikki.org: Countless volunteer lexicographers and the Kaikki extraction project made the rich dictionary data that powers Jolito’s offline translation and verb inflection assistance. Content licensed under CC BY-SA 3.0.
  • HermitDave & OpenSubtitles: Real-world subtitle word frequency data that helps Jolito highlight the colloquial phrases and everyday words people actually speak on the street.

🎨 Typography & Design

  • Mathieu Triay: Designer of Bricolage Grotesque, the delightfully expressive, rhythmic typeface that gives Jolito its bold CDMX modernist character. Distributed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.

🎙️ Spoken Voices

  • Microsoft Neural Speech: High-definition Mexican Spanish voices (Dalia & Jorge) and English prompts (Jenny & Guy) that give Jolito its natural spoken cadence.
  • Browser Speech Engines: The device-native speech synthesis engines built into iOS, macOS, and Android that keep pronunciation instant and reliable even when completely offline.

🛠️ The Open-Source Engine

Jolito operates local-first, offline, and with zero tracking thanks to these foundation libraries:

  • sql.js: SQLite compiled to WebAssembly, keeping cards and review history securely stored directly in your browser. (MIT)
  • fflate: Super-fast ZIP parsing for Anki deck packages. (MIT)
  • React, Capacitor, & Zod: The reactive UI, native iOS container, and strict runtime contract validations. (MIT)

❤️ Community & Inspiration

Special thanks to the patient Spanish teachers at International House Mexico in Condesa, and to Mexico City itself—the street stalls, metro rides, and daily neighborhood chats that inspired this project.
